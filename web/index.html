Concept 01 — the core problem

The chase you can't see

A chase camera looks forward; the boulders are behind. If the player can't read which lanes are claimed, every hit feels unfair. Four devices put the rear threat on screen without ever turning the camera around.

  1. The lane ribbon

    Three pips across the top, welded to the lanes below them. Jade = clear, amber = a boulder is drifting in, red = claimed and grinding. It is the only pure-UI element in the game, and it is the one you actually play off.

  2. Shadows arriving first

    The key light sits behind the runner, so each boulder throws a long shadow past the Professor and onto the slabs ahead. The shadow's width is its distance: a hard-edged black bar creeping up your lane means it is one second out.

  3. Dust at the frame edge

    A bottom vignette of ochre grit, tinted red and tightened as the nearest boulder closes. Peripheral, never blocking — you feel it before you read it.

  4. The shoulder glance

    Every ~20 seconds, or on the first hit of a grind, the camera dips and rolls 12° for 0.4s to sell the mass behind you. A flourish with a job: it re-establishes the geography for free.

Concept 02 — the cast

Blocked out from primitives

Every model below is boxes, cylinders and icosahedra assembled in code — no downloaded mesh, no texture, no UVs. Chunky Kenney-scale volumes, flat-shaded facets, one warm key and a cool bounce. Drag to spin any of them.

The crack

The lash is a chain of nine tapering segments, each parented to the last; a sine wave travelling down the chain straightens it at 0.18s and snaps the tip past the arm. The hit frame is the straight frame — that is what the timing window is anchored to.

Concept 03 — the damage model

Grind, don't splat

A boulder that kills on contact makes a twitch game. A boulder that grinds makes a positioning game: contact starts a drain that only stops when you reach a clear lane, so every hit is a decision, not a death.

Concept 04 — the world

One 12-metre module

The causeway is a single 12m prefab, recycled six at a time: three slab lanes, a grooved divider, kerbs, pillar pairs, torches, canopy. Seeded variation means each module is deterministic but never twins with its neighbour.

Module · 12 m · ~1,900 tris

Temple causeway

Lane slabs alternate two sand tones at 2.86m intervals, which gives the speed read at 20 m/s that a flat floor loses. Everything above 3m of height is decoration and gets culled first on low-end devices.

Concept 06 — the hand

One thumb, portrait, no buttons

The whole control surface is the screen. Nothing sits in the bottom 12% except the run itself, because that is where the thumb rests and where a phone's home bar lives.

  1. Swipe ← / → : change lane

    Threshold 28px, buffered 120ms so a swipe during a lane change queues the next one. Input is read anywhere on screen.

  2. Tap : crack the whip

    Anywhere, any time, 0.30s cooldown. No aim — the whip hits the player's lane, which keeps the decision about when, not where.

  3. Swipe ↓ : slide

    Ducks low vines and hanging webs for 0.7s. Also the panic button under a low arch — and it does not stop a grind.

  4. Haptics carry the grind

    A 30ms tick per HP segment lost, escalating. On a tablet without haptics the screen edge pulses instead.
